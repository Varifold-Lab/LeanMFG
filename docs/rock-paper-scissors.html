1. The model

The population chooses among Rock, Paper, and Scissors. A representative player sees the population mix m and chooses a personal mix p.

Each mix is a triple of rational probabilities. Its three entries are nonnegative and sum to 1; these conditions are proof fields of the Lean Distribution structure.

Unilateral deviation

Changing p does not change m. This distinction is built into expectedReward p m.

expectedReward(p, m) = Σa ∈ {R,P,S} p(a) · reward(a, m)

Using the everyday rule “win = +1, loss = −1, tie = 0,” the three pure-action rewards are:

Rockm(S) − m(P)
Paperm(R) − m(S)
Scissorsm(P) − m(R)
Sign convention

Learning in Mean Field Games: A Survey, §2.1 Example 2, describes a static rock–paper–scissors MFG with the opposite cyclic reward signs. This page follows the everyday win/loss convention; its formulas are not copied from the survey.

2. What counts as equilibrium?

At equilibrium, the representative player uses the same mix as the population. No other rational mix may improve their reward while the population stays fixed:

∀ p, expectedReward(p, m) ≤ expectedReward(m, m)

An ε-equilibrium requires ε ≥ 0 and allows an improvement of at most ε:

∀ p, expectedReward(p, m) ≤ expectedReward(m, m) + ε

Population consistency is exact in both definitions: the personal strategy being certified is m itself.

3. The executable certificate

bestResponse m compares all three pure rewards and returns a maximizing action. Ties favor Rock, then Paper, then Scissors.

exploitability(m) = reward(bestResponse(m), m) − expectedReward(m, m)

This is exactly the most a single player can gain by changing strategy. It is always nonnegative.

checkEpsilonEquilibrium m ε is an executable Boolean check of ε ≥ 0 and exploitability m ≤ ε. No abstract optimizer or noncomputable definition is used for these programs.

4. What connects the program to the definition?

  1. reward_le_bestResponse proves that the action returned by the actual bestResponse program beats or ties every pure action.
  2. expectedReward_le_bestResponse uses nonnegative probabilities summing to 1 to bound every rational mixed strategy by that chosen pure action.
  3. exploitability_nonneg applies the mixed bound to m itself.
  4. checkEpsilonEquilibrium_iff proves that the Boolean checker accepts exactly the quantified ε-equilibria. The reverse direction tests the pure strategy returned by bestResponse.
Why the uniform mix is unique

expectedReward_self shows self-play has payoff 0. At an exact equilibrium, each pure-action reward is therefore at most 0. The three rewards sum to 0, so each must equal 0. The payoff equations and probabilities summing to 1 then force m(R) = m(P) = m(S) = 1/3. Lean proves both that this uniform mix is an equilibrium and that every equilibrium equals it.

6. Run it

lake exe cache get
lake build

lake exe cache get fetches precompiled mathlib artifacts. lake build checks all proofs and evaluates every #guard; a failed guard fails the build.

Scope: rational distributions on three actions in a static game. This example verifies best responses and equilibrium certification. It does not implement an equilibrium iteration, prove iteration convergence, or cover dynamic mean field games.
