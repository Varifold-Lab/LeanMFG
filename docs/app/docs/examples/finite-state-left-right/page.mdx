# Finite-state Left/Right

A one-decision mean field game built on a reusable finite-state model. The worked example follows the Left/Right environment in the [MFGLib Quickstart](https://mfglib.readthedocs.io/en/stable/quickstart.html), using rational probabilities for exact Lean computation.

Everyone starts in the central state. Action Left moves an agent to the left state; action Right moves her to the right state. Against a fixed terminal population law `m`, payoffs are

$$
r(\mathrm{Left},m)=-m(\mathrm{Left}),
\qquad r(\mathrm{Right},m)=-2m(\mathrm{Right}).
$$

## Model and population update

A <SourceLink path="LeanMFG/Model/FiniteState/OneStep.lean">policy</SourceLink> assigns a rational action distribution to each state. `inducedTerminal` computes the population law produced by the candidate policy. `value G π m` evaluates one individual's policy `π` against a **fixed** `m`, including when that individual deviates. The model permits stochastic transitions, stage rewards, and terminal rewards.

The shared `update` operator takes a current distribution and policy. The <SourceLink path="LeanMFG/Verification/FiniteState/Conservation.lean">conservation theorems</SourceLink> prove from its weighted transition sums that all destination masses stay nonnegative and their total remains one. `evolve` repeats the update for any policy sequence, with the same guarantee at every step. The assumptions are normalized transitions and a closed population; equilibrium is not required.

## Best response and equilibrium check

The <SourceLink path="LeanMFG/Algorithm/FiniteState/OneStep.lean">algorithm</SourceLink> selects a maximizing action in each state, computes exploitability, and checks ε-equilibrium. Its <SourceLink path="LeanMFG/Verification/FiniteState/OneStep.lean">proofs</SourceLink> establish that the selected action is optimal, no mixed policy scores higher against the fixed population, exploitability is nonnegative, and the Boolean checker is equivalent to the quantified equilibrium definition.

In the <SourceLink path="LeanMFG/Examples/FiniteState/LeftRight.lean">worked example</SourceLink>, everyone chooses Left with probability `2/3` and Right with probability `1/3`. The terminal population has the same left/right masses, so both actions score `−2/3`. The policy is proved to be an exact equilibrium. If everyone chooses Left, deviating Right improves payoff by `1`.

Run `lake build` for the general proofs and executable `#guard` checks. This game has one decision and one transition. Its reusable population update accepts multiple steps, but a multistep Bellman equilibrium solver and continuous-state population flow are not implemented here.
